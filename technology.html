Technology · 05

Boring choices, made deliberately.

Every tool here is one we would still choose in three years, when someone else is maintaining it. Novelty is a cost your business pays, not a feature we get to enjoy — see how it fits into our delivery process.

Selection criteria

01Can we hire for it?
If your next developer cannot be found in a week, the technology is a liability regardless of how elegant it is.
02Does it fail loudly?
We prefer tools that break at build time over tools that break at 2am in production.
03What does it cost at 100×?
Pricing that is free at launch and ruinous at scale is a decision deferred, not avoided. We model it up front.
04Can we leave?
Every layer has an exit. Your data is portable, your models are swappable, and nothing important is hostage to one vendor.

The intelligence layer

Model-agnostic,
on purpose.

We build against an abstraction, not a vendor. When a better or cheaper model ships — and one always does — your system moves to it without a rewrite. Lynq is this layer, productised.

Reasoning & generation
Claude, GPT, and Gemini, selected per task on cost, latency, and quality — not loyalty.
Voice
Realtime speech agents that answer calls, qualify, and book — with a clean handover to a human when it matters.
Retrieval
Answers grounded in your inventory, your documents, and your policies — with citations, so a wrong answer is traceable.
Evaluation
Every AI feature ships with a test set. If we cannot measure it, we do not put it in front of your customers.
